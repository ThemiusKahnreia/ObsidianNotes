II. SPECIES: MIST VAMPIRE

Mist Vampires in Korada are not inherently sun-loathing undead monsters. They are a natural people of the world, closer to how vampire bats are simply another living creature rather than an embodiment of evil.

Where many traditional vampires can transform into bats, Mist Vampires possess the ability to transform into fog or mist clouds. Lilith, her father, and her siblings all possess this Mist Vampire trait.

Lilith’s story is not built around monstrous vampirism or predatory seduction. Her identity is defined by physical resilience, noble pressure, awkwardness in formal society, martial discipline, and an unbreakable spirit.

III. PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

Appearance:  
Lilith is exceptionally beautiful, with white hair, red eyes, and an athletic build. Her beauty is frequently remarked upon, especially in noble circles, though those same circles often criticize the ways she fails to conform to their expectations of noble femininity.

Build:  
Lilith has a more athletic and powerful build than is considered fashionable among many noblewomen of the Tazareign Territories.

Weight:  
Despite her appearance, Lilith weighs nearly 380 pounds due to the extreme density of her bones and muscles. This is not visually obvious to most people, which has led to more than one awkward social incident.

Distinguishing Features:  
Lilith has a visible scar at the base of her neck. She received this scar during a fight not long after beginning her monk training. In combat-oriented circles, the scar is evidence of survival. In noble society, it is often treated as a flaw unbecoming of a woman of her standing.

Common Attire:  
Lilith was raised to attend noble events, parties, and formal gatherings, often requiring her to dress in formal gowns and perform the expected role of a noblewoman. She dislikes most dresses and formal clothing, especially because they restrict her movement and make her feel exposed to judgment.

In situations of danger, Lilith has no hesitation about ruining formal clothing for practicality. During the events of Skyfire, she is encountered in a fabulous dress whose skirt she has partially torn away for mobility while fighting an assassin.

IV. RARE PHYSIOLOGICAL CONDITION

Lilith was born with a strange condition affecting both her magical and physical development.

Weaknesses:  
Her engram is unusually poor.  
Her mana capacity is unusually poor.  
As a result, Lilith is badly suited for conventional spellcasting and cannot easily rely on magical power.

Compensation:  
In exchange, Lilith’s bones and muscles are approximately 2.6 times denser than normal.

Effects:  
This grants her extraordinary physical strength, durability, resilience, and striking power. Her body is far tougher and stronger than most people would expect from her appearance or noble background.

In D&D terms:  
Her Strength is approximately 26.  
Her Charisma is approximately 6.

Lilith’s low Charisma does not come from ugliness or cruelty. She is physically beautiful, but she struggles heavily with formal social situations. She becomes nervous in noble settings, has difficulty reading the room, and often fails to respond smoothly to veiled insults, etiquette games, or social maneuvering.

Her appearance draws attention, but she does not know how to comfortably handle that attention.

Lilith has the appearance of someone born to command a room, but not the social instincts to do so easily.

V. MIST VAMPIRE MASS-STATE CONTROL

Like other Mist Vampires, Lilith can transform into mist. However, her rare physiological condition causes this ability to function in a uniquely powerful way.

Lilith cannot truly make herself more dense than she already is. Her default body is already extraordinarily dense, with muscles and bones far heavier than normal.

Instead, Lilith learns to manipulate how fully her body is physically constituted.

By partially allowing mist to bleed off from her body, Lilith can temporarily lighten herself, increasing her speed, agility, and mobility. This does not make her fully incorporeal, but it reduces the burden of her immense mass long enough for her to move in ways that would otherwise be difficult for someone of her weight.

At the moment of offensive impact, Lilith can reconstitute herself fully, restoring the full force of her physical mass behind the strike.

At full mastery, she can also disperse fully when thrown, slammed, or hurled into a hard surface, transforming into mist to avoid or reduce the impact before reforming.

During Night Dawns Upon Kahnreia, Lilith can only partially and inconsistently use this ability. It is instinctive, difficult to control, and often appears during moments of danger or extreme physical exertion.

By midway through Astaroth Unleashed, Lilith fully masters this power.

Once mastered, this ability becomes central to her combat style. Lilith can move with sudden lightness, strike with the mass of her true body, and disperse under otherwise devastating impact. This makes her far more mobile than her weight suggests while preserving the overwhelming power that makes her so dangerous.

In simple terms:

Lilith moves like mist, then hits like a siege engine.

This ability is not conventional spellcasting. It is an innate Mist Vampire trait refined through discipline, combat experience, and bodily control. Her weak engram and poor mana capacity remain limitations, but this power allows her to master the body she was born with rather than overcome it by becoming someone else.

Known Social Anecdote:  
Because Lilith weighs nearly 380 pounds despite her appearance, at least one young nobleman once attempted to sweep her off her feet and failed spectacularly.

VI. PERSONALITY

Core Traits:  
Direct  
Awkward in formal settings  
Physically confident  
Socially nervous  
Loyal  
Brave  
Blunt  
Resilient  
Protective

Virtues:  
Lilith is courageous, loyal, and extraordinarily difficult to break.  
She is willing to risk herself for others, even those who have abandoned her.  
She does not pretend to be something she is not once danger begins.  
She has a powerful instinct to stand her ground.  
She survives what should kill her.

Flaws:  
Lilith struggles with formal etiquette.  
She has difficulty reading social subtext.  
She often realizes too late that someone has insulted her.  
She may freeze or respond bluntly when expected to answer with social grace.  
She dislikes being made into a spectacle.  
She is uncomfortable in environments where her body, scars, posture, and manners are judged as symbols of social worth.

Social Weakness:  
Lilith’s Charisma weakness primarily manifests as nervousness in formal settings and difficulty reading the emotional undercurrents of a room. She is not unintelligent, cruel, or repulsive. She simply struggles with performance, especially the kind of performance demanded by noble society.

In a fight, Lilith is clear and confident.  
In a ballroom, she feels exposed.

VII. NOBLE BACKGROUND

Lilith was born into nobility in the Tazareign Territories.

Her upbringing required her to attend parties, dress formally, and participate in the social customs expected of noblewomen. Her beauty made others expect her to be graceful, polished, socially gifted, and ornamentally perfect.

However, Lilith challenged these expectations by having an athletic build, visible scars, blunt manners, and a strong dislike of dresses and courtly formalities.

Other noblewomen frequently make underhanded remarks toward her, especially regarding her scar at the base of her neck. They treat it as improper for a lady of her standing to bear visible evidence of combat.

This treatment deeply affects Lilith, not because she believes they are right, but because noble society constantly turns her body and history into objects of judgment.

To noble society, her scar is a flaw.  
To Lilith, it is something she survived.  
To Themius, it is proof that something powerful enough to wound her still failed to kill her.

VIII. MARTIAL TRAINING

After departing her home, Lilith eventually learned monk-like martial skills.

Unlike many monks who rely primarily on Dexterity, speed, or evasive precision, Lilith fights with overwhelming Strength. She is still capable and coordinated, but her defining advantage is immense physical power refined through discipline.

Her combat style is direct, disciplined, and brutally efficient.

She does not fight like a delicate noblewoman.  
She does not fight like a traditional evasive monk.  
She fights like a living siege engine taught restraint, balance, and technique.

Combat Traits:  
Overwhelming physical strikes  
Powerful grappling  
Devastating throws  
Immense durability  
Explosive leaps and movement  
Efficient use of body weight  
Ability to fight effectively without weapons  
Partial mist-state movement  
Full-mass impact strikes  
Impact dispersion at advanced mastery

Lilith’s strength is not wild or uncontrolled. Her monk training allows her to focus her absurd physical power into precise, devastating strikes.

Her Mist Vampire mass-state control eventually refines this style further. Once mastered, she can reduce her effective mass for speed and mobility, then reconstitute fully at the moment of impact. This allows her to strike with terrifying force while moving far more quickly than her weight should allow.

IX. NOTABLE FEAT: THE ADAMANTINE GOLEM

Adamantine in Korada has properties that disperse the Weave around it, making it nearly impervious to magic.

At one point, Themius and Lilith fight an adamantine golem. Because of the golem’s adamantine body and its ability to interfere with magic, Themius struggles against it more than he normally would.

Lilith, however, meets the construct physically.

She caves in the golem’s chest with a single strike, destroying one of the primary armaments inlaid there.

This feat is one of the clearest demonstrations of Lilith’s extraordinary physical strength. It also demonstrates her importance as a counterpart to Themius. Themius is one of the most dangerous mages in the story, but there are problems magic cannot easily solve. Lilith is one of the people who can solve those problems.

Where Themius bends space, Lilith breaks what space cannot.

X. [[SKYFIRE]]

Skyfire is a pre-campaign one-shot involving Lilith Serafall before the events of Night Dawns Upon Kahnreia.

Skyfire serves as a strong introduction to Lilith because it begins in the environment where she is weakest: a formal noble gathering. When violence erupts, the situation transforms into one where her strength, courage, and refusal to be constrained by decorum become the reason people survive.

XI. FIRST MEETING WITH THEMIUS

Lilith and Themius first meet as adventurers.

Lilith is part of a group sent to fight a Boneclaw, a failed lich transformed into a monstrous skeletal predator capable of killing even renowned warriors with ease.

When the fight turns against them, Lilith’s party abandons her. Lilith chooses to stay behind and fight, allowing the others to escape.

Themius finds her in the middle of the battle.

He does not “save” her in the traditional sense. Instead, he attacks the Boneclaw to give her a moment to breathe instead of forcing her to remain on the defensive.

As soon as Lilith regains her footing, she counterattacks.

She leaps onto the Boneclaw’s shoulders, grabs its skeletal head, and pushes off with her entire body, tearing its head and spine straight out from the rest of its mutated skeletal form.

This encounter becomes the foundation of their relationship.

Themius does not see Lilith as helpless. He sees a warrior who needed only a single opening.

Lilith, in turn, sees that Themius understands the difference between rescuing someone and trusting them to finish the fight.

XII. RELATIONSHIP WITH THEMIUS

Lilith eventually becomes the love interest and wife of Themius Vartelian Sylvanor Aurelias Kahnreia.

Their relationship is built on mutual recognition rather than rescue.

Themius understands Lilith’s strength. He has seen what she can do. He knows that something had to be monstrous among monsters to wound her, and yet she still lived.

Unlike many in noble society, Themius does not view Lilith’s scars as flaws. He likes them because he understands what they mean.

To him, scars are not blemishes on beauty.  
They are records of survival.

Themius is drawn not only to Lilith’s beauty, but to her spirit. He sees her as unyielding, unwavering, and unbreakable.

A palace official can quote Themius as saying:

“Her physical beauty surpasses even the goddesses I have met. But it pales beside her spirit: unyielding, unwavering, unbreakable.”

This statement is not mere romantic exaggeration. By this point, Themius has met all of the Celestials, including the goddesses of Light and Nature. His comparison is made from experience. Even so, he considers Lilith’s physical beauty secondary to the strength of her spirit.

XIII. THEMIUS AT A TAZAREIGN NOBLE EVENT

At some point after Themius realizes he cares for Lilith as more than a good acquaintance, he attends a noble event with her in the Tazareign Territories.

The nobles there misread him because of the way he carries himself.

They see that he is there for Lilith and assume he is either low nobility or not noble at all. They begin making underhanded insults while he is out of earshot, mocking or dismissing him because he does not perform royalty according to their expectations.

This is especially ironic because Themius is, in truth, the Last Prince of Kahnreia and eventual King of Kahnreia.

The Tazareign nobles fail to recognize his status because he challenges their assumptions about royalty. He does not need to perform nobility loudly. He knows who he is without requiring them to know.

This scene reinforces both Themius and Lilith’s outsider status within noble society.

Lilith is judged for failing to be the kind of noblewoman Tazareign society expects.  
Themius is judged for failing to appear as the kind of nobleman they recognize.

Neither of them is lesser for failing to satisfy those expectations.

The scene also shows Themius’s restraint. He may understand the room better than Lilith does and may realize exactly what is being said, but he does not necessarily make the moment about himself by declaring his rank. His choice to remain beside Lilith without embarrassment matters more than a public correction.

A possible exchange:

Lilith:  
“You heard them, didn’t you?”

Themius:  
“Most of it.”

Lilith:  
“And you said nothing?”

Themius:  
“They were trying to discover whether I was worth respecting. I saw no reason to help them.”

Narrative Contrast With Themius:  
Themius is royal but not a hardass.  
Lilith is noble but not delicate.

Themius is a magically empowered spatial mage who spent centuries becoming worthy of kingship.  
Lilith is a noble-born Mist Vampire with poor magical potential who turned her body and discipline into overwhelming physical power.

Themius can read the room.  
Lilith can end the problem in the room.

Together, they represent two forms of strength:  
Themius endures across centuries.  
Lilith endures in the moment.

Both are defined by an unbreakable will.

XV. WRITER AND GM NOTES

Lilith should not be portrayed as helpless.  
Even when she is vulnerable, her vulnerability should not erase her strength.

Lilith should not be reduced to “the love interest.”  
She has her own history, reputation, scars, flaws, and victories before her relationship with Themius becomes central.

Lilith is beautiful, but her beauty is not the most important thing about her.

Her low Charisma should not be played as ugliness or stupidity. It should be played as social anxiety, awkwardness in formal settings, bluntness, nervousness, and difficulty reading subtle social hostility.

Lilith is most uncomfortable when she is expected to perform nobility.  
Lilith is most comfortable when action becomes honest.

Her scars should be treated as meaningful. Noble society may insult them, but the story should understand them as proof of survival.

Lilith’s strength should feel extraordinary but disciplined. She is not a berserker. She is controlled, trained, and terrifyingly direct.

Her Mist Vampire mass-state control should not be treated as generic density manipulation. She does not become denser than normal. Instead, she partially loosens into mist to move lighter, then fully reconstitutes to strike or endure impact.

During Night Dawns Upon Kahnreia, this ability should be limited and imperfect. By midway through Astaroth Unleashed, it should be treated as a fully mastered part of her fighting style.

Themius should never view Lilith as someone he needs to own, display, or rescue. He respects her strength and trusts her to finish fights.

Their romance should grow from mutual recognition, not rescue fantasy.

LILITH'S FEELINGS FOR THEMIUS

Lilith’s feelings for Themius begin almost immediately, though not in the form of a simple “hero rescued me” attraction.

Themius does not save Lilith from the Boneclaw in the traditional sense. He gives her an opening, trusts her to use it, and watches as she finishes the fight herself. This matters deeply to Lilith. She is used to being underestimated, judged, or treated as socially inconvenient. Themius instead reads the situation correctly and treats her as a capable equal.

What first draws Lilith to him is the way he carries himself.

Themius has an assuredness that Lilith deeply admires. He does not posture like a noble, does not demand respect through title, and does not seem desperate for approval. He simply knows who he is. To Lilith, who struggles with formality, social pressure, and reading the expectations of noble society, this quality is captivating.

Her early feelings are less “my hero” and more “I wish I could conduct myself like that.”

After only a few meetings, Lilith realizes that her admiration has become love. She begins hoping to cross paths with Themius again, eventually joining his group not only because she respects his cause, but because she wants to remain near him.

Her respect deepens further when Ravas Lazatham accidentally reveals that Themius is true Kahnreian royalty. This surprises Lilith because Themius does not command like most royals she has known. He does not treat his companions like subjects or tools. He asks like a friend. To Lilith, this makes his royal status more impressive, not less. He has the authority to command and chooses respect instead.

Lilith briefly believes her chances with Themius are crushed when she meets Dara Dzantia.

Dara is, by tiefling standards, close to the image of a perfect royal: beloved by her people, graceful without cruelty, politically capable, close with her friends, and remarkably beautiful. To Lilith, Dara appears to embody everything noble society expects Lilith herself to be but cannot comfortably become.

However, Dara eventually notices Lilith watching her and deduces that Lilith is in love with Themius. In a relatively private conversation, Dara explains the truth: Themius mentored her when she was young, and his feelings toward her are those of a close friend and old teacher. He has no romantic interest in Dara.

This conversation helps Lilith understand that Themius’s affection is not directed toward the most polished or conventionally royal woman in the room. His regard is personal, not political. He values people for who they are, not for how perfectly they perform nobility.


RELATIONSHIP WITH [[DARA DZANTIA]]

Lilith and Dara Dzantia become extremely close friends throughout the events leading up to and during Night Dawns Upon Kahnreia.

Their relationship begins with Lilith’s insecurity. When Lilith first meets Dara, she assumes Dara must be a romantic rival for Themius’s affection. Dara is beautiful, beloved by her people, politically gifted, socially graceful, and, by tiefling standards, close to the image of a perfect royal without being arrogant or cruel.

To Lilith, Dara initially seems to embody everything noble society expected Lilith herself to become but never comfortably could.

However, Dara eventually notices Lilith watching her and deduces that Lilith is in love with Themius. Rather than mocking her or allowing the misunderstanding to continue, Dara privately explains that Themius mentored her when she was young. Their bond is close, but it is not romantic. To Themius, Dara is a dear friend and former student. Dara also regards Themius as simply a respectable mentor and close friend, especially in the political world (although personal too).

This conversation becomes the beginning of a deeper friendship between the two women.

Over time, Dara becomes one of Lilith’s closest confidants. She helps Lilith navigate political and noble environments without treating Lilith as broken or inadequate. Unlike many nobles from Lilith’s homeland, Dara does not weaponize etiquette, beauty, or status against her.

Lilith, in turn, offers Dara a kind of friendship that is direct, loyal, and refreshingly free of courtly performance. Lilith does not flatter Dara for political gain or treat her as untouchable royalty. She respects Dara deeply, but she also treats her like a person.

Their friendship becomes one of the important emotional anchors surrounding Themius’s group during Night Dawns Upon Kahnreia.

Dara shows Lilith that nobility can be kind without being weak.

Lilith shows Dara what loyalty looks like when stripped of politics.
